Why a year feels shorter at every birthday

A year at five is a fifth of everything you have ever known. A year at fifty is a fiftieth. The calendar counts time evenly; memory does not. Psychologists have described this for more than a century as proportional, logarithmic perception — and it is why the first two decades of life feel enormous while the decades after forty blur together.

Day of My Life models that curve. It weights each stage of life — early childhood, school, working life, retirement — and integrates a logarithmic density to build a subjective timeline that runs fast where life feels slow, and slow where life feels fast.
